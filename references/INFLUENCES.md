# Influences

Choosing which writers a world is built out of, and weighting them so the result is interwoven rather
than striped.

Read with `VOICE.md`. The influence set is part of the world's law; this file says how to pick it and
how to run it.

---

## Part 1 — Choosing the set

### Do not ask who their influences are

It is the same failure as asking for twenty places. It demands a search of everything they have ever
read plus a ranking, on the spot, with an implied correct answer, and what it returns is the writers
they think they should say. Somebody's stated influences and the writers actually visible in their
taste are routinely different people.

### Three ways in, in order of yield

**1. Derive it, then let them veto.** By the time this comes up you have their captures and probably
a test paragraph and their notes on it. Propose four or five writers with a sentence each on *what
that writer would own here*, drawn from what you have seen them respond to.

> Going on the captures and what you struck out of that draft, I'd guess this world wants something
> like: [A] for how scenes end, [B] for dialogue, [C] for the way machinery gets explained,
> [D] for whether the reality holds still. Which of those is wrong?

**Asking which is wrong, rather than whether it is right, is the whole trick.** Vetoing is easy and
immediate. Confirming is vague and produces a shrug.

**2. Ask what they re-read.** Not what is best, and not what they admire. Re-reading is behavioural
evidence and it is retrievable without a search.

Useful neighbours: what they have started five times and never finished but will not give away, what
they would defend in an argument, and what they loved at nineteen and are now embarrassed by. The
embarrassing one is often the most visible in their actual sentences.

**3. Name the job, then cast it.** If a writer's name will not come, ask about the function and fill
it later:

> When a scene ends in this world, does it land on an image, a line of dialogue, or does it just
> stop?

That answer selects an influence whether or not either of you can name one yet.

### Rules for the chooser

- **Four to eight is the working range.** Under four and the prose has no tension. Over eight and
  most of them are decorative, since only two or three are audible at once (see Part 2).
- **Pick for conflict, not for agreement.** A set that agrees produces one flavour. The set should
  contain at least one writer who strips and one who accumulates, because the contest between them
  is where the register actually lives.
- **A living writer is a licence question.** Fine as an influence, since a channel is not a text, but
  do not let the prose become imitation of a specific living author's style as a selling point.
- **Write down what each one is *for*.** An influence in the list with no channel assigned will
  either do nothing or be used as an excuse.
- **The set is law and changes only by the author, in writing, dated.** An engine may propose an
  addition. It may not adopt one.

## Part 2 — Running the dial

*Mechanism adapted from a working world's `INFLUENCE-DIAL.md`. The general form holds; the specific
assignments are that world's and are marked as an example.*

### A percentage is a share of contested decisions, not a share of text

Every sentence poses choices: stop or continue, explain or withhold, name the feeling or leave it,
close the scene or let it run. **A mix is the standing answer to who wins those.** It is not a
schedule, and it never means the first quarter of the piece is one writer.

### Interweaving works because influences own different channels

They are not voices competing for one job. They are layers operating simultaneously in the same
paragraph. Assign every influence a channel and the mix stops being arithmetic:

| Channel | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Architecture** | How the piece is shaped and where the turn falls |
| **The ending** | Whether it lands on an image, a line, a verdict, or nothing |
| **Texture and length** | Where the word count goes, and what gets explained |
| **The object** | Whether things are examined for meaning or for mechanism |
| **Dialogue** | How people talk, and how much they evade |
| **The brake** | What stops the prose reaching for a summarising emotion |
| **Interiority** | How long attention rests on something, and whether it resolves |
| **Pressure** | How much moral heat a person under strain gives off |
| **The apparatus** | How institutions behave toward a person |
| **Ontology** | Whether the reality holds still |

Two influences may share a channel if the distinction is stated. *One explains how a thing works,
the other examines what a thing means* is a real distinction and worth writing down; *both are good
on objects* is not.

### Ambient, punctual, gesture

**A low-weight influence is not diluted everywhere. It is wholly present in a small part.**

- **25% and above: ambient.** Present in most paragraphs, shaping ordinary sentences.
- **10 to 24%: punctual.** One or two concentrated placements, chosen for where only that influence
  can do the job.
- **Below 10%: one gesture, once, or leave it out.** Under roughly 8% an influence is not
  distinguishable from noise, and naming it claims a precision the prose cannot carry.

At any moment only two or three dials are audible. That is correct, and it is what stops the piece
sounding like a committee.

### Some channels are binary

**Ontology and architecture barely take fine weighting.** A reality either destabilises or it does
not. A piece either has the turn or it has not. **Texture, dialogue and interiority are continuous**
and weight well. When a mix assigns a small number to a binary channel, round it to zero or to
present, and say which.

### Conflicts are where the ratio bites

The ratio is only meaningful where two influences want opposite things. Name the contests explicitly
for the world's own set. The recurring ones:

- **The stripper against the accumulators.** The ratio decides how often the brake wins, and the
  stripped moments should be placed where they cost most: dialogue, paragraph ends, the last line.
- **The architect against the dweller.** One wants the turn to land and move on, the other wants to
  stay. Whoever owns architecture wins by default, and the other's passage sits inside a structure it
  does not control.
- **Mechanism against fable on the ending.** A mechanism wants a conclusion; a fable wants an image.

### The register is not a dial

Influences render **through** the world's register, never as pastiche. A mix allocates influence; it
does not touch identity. **A mix that totals under 100 is not missing anything: the remainder is the
register, which is always there.**

### It is composable, not measurable

Prose can be written to a specification. It cannot be scored back to one. Handed a finished piece and
asked *what did this come out at*, any number produced is invented. **The dial is a composition
instruction. It is never a metric and must not be reported as one.**

### Worked example, from the world this was adapted from

A mix of 28 architecture-and-ending / 16 dialogue-and-brake / 32 texture / 14 interiority:

- **Texture at 32, ambient.** The air of the piece. Mechanisms explained at length and with evident
  pleasure, and knowable.
- **Architecture at 28, ambient in structure and decisive twice.** Invisible sentence to sentence.
  Owns the turn and the last image.
- **Dialogue and brake at 16, punctual.** Every line of speech, and the ends of scenes.
- **Interiority at 14, punctual and singular.** One sustained paragraph near the two-thirds mark, on
  something that does not resolve. Nowhere else.
- **Remainder 10:** the register.

Note what the absences do. With nothing on the apparatus channel, institutions in that mix must be
comprehensible rather than oppressive, and the harm has to come from the machine working correctly.
**Reading the zeroes is as informative as reading the weights**, and a mix should always be stated
with its zeroes visible.

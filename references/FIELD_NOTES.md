## 2026-11-11: a noir anthology co-written with Claude, from IT incidents

Source: r/WritingWithAI, u/bcRIPster, "I co-authored a noir anthology with Claude", posted
2026-11-10. The stories are at https://kitchencloset.com/realstuff/tech_support_trials/seven_layer/

What happened. An infrastructure engineer with thirty years of incidents turned them into seven
noir stories, one per OSI layer. He supplied the incidents, the technical detail and the voice,
and Claude shaped the prose. His claim is that Claude could write noir but could not tell which
detail in an incident was the one that cracked it, and that this judgment was the whole job. In
the comments he says the structure was in his drafts and notes before Claude saw them.

A reader asked whether a parallel in one story came from his incident notes or emerged while
working with Claude. He answered from memory. The post scored 6 up and 11 down.

What it means for Yildun.

1. The question the reader asked is the question the AMR log answers with a record. Without a
   log, the author can only assert where an idea came from, and the downvotes suggest readers do
   not take that on trust.
2. His "editorial judgment" is what accept, modify and reject decisions capture, one decision at
   a time, with the reason in the note.
3. "Fiction grounded in professional experience" is the rooted case: each piece traces to a
   human origin, here a real incident. A provenance map would show that per story.

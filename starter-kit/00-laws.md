# 00 · The Laws

**Template.** Replace the content with your world's own laws. Keep the shape: a short numbered
list, locked, that every entry can be checked against. Six to twelve is the useful range. Fewer
and they do not constrain; more and nobody holds them in their head.

A law is not a preference. It is a constraint that makes decisions for you at three in the morning
and that you would notice being broken. If a candidate law would never rule anything out, it is
flavour text, and it belongs in the voice file instead.

## What good laws do

Each one should close off an easy, attractive, generic option. That is the test. Write them as
prohibitions or as forced pairings, not as aspirations.

- Aspiration, useless: *the world should feel lived-in.*
- Law, useful: *every legend carries a Myth and a Practical version, and the practical one is
  true and is always cheaper, smellier and more clerical.*

## Structural laws worth stealing

These are not about any particular world and have earned their place:

1. **Nothing is rootless.** Every entry traces, through however many hops, to something a human
   supplied: a capture, or a law the author wrote. The machine may expand the world without limit
   and travel arbitrarily far from any seed; what it may not do is generate from nothing but its
   own output. Recorded in `derives_from`. See `01-structure.md`.
2. **Contradiction is a register, not a multiverse.** When two parts of the world disagree, tag
   the weaker one (*bad record*, *false memory*, *rumor*, *repaired lie*) rather than inventing
   parallel realities. See `references/METHOD.md`.
3. **Every object is a joke and evidence at once.** An object that is only funny is set dressing.
   One that is only evidence is a prop. It has to be both.
4. **No glamorous destinations.** Name the second-rate version of anywhere. Specificity is cheap
   at the edges and expensive at the centre.
5. **The uncertainty is the fiction.** When you do not know a real fact and cannot check it,
   that gap becomes a bad record inside the world. It never becomes a confident invention.

## The emotional centre

One or two sentences naming what the world is actually about, in terms of people rather than
plot. Every entry should bend toward it, even faintly. Write this last, after twenty entries
exist, because you will be wrong about it before then.
